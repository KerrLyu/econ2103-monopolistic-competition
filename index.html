How to read the graph

Navy: demand (D). Dashed amber: marginal revenue (MR), which falls twice as fast as D. Red: marginal cost (MC). Teal: average total cost (ATC). The shop produces where MR = MC (the black ring), then charges the price on D at that output (the dot). The box has height P − ATC and width Q*: green is profit, red is loss. With the comparison on, purple marks what perfect competition would give: P = MC = minimum ATC at Q = 4. The brackets show excess capacity (how far Q* falls short of 4) and the markup (P − MC).

Numbers behind the graph

Cost model from the lecture: MC = 2 + Q and ATC = 8/Q + 2 + 0.5Q, so ATC is lowest (at $6) when Q = 4. Demand is P = A − BQ with MR = A − 2BQ. The slider moves A down and B toward 0.25 as similar shops enter. With one shop, D is P = 14 − Q. At 6 shops, D is P = 6.90 − 0.25Q and just touches ATC. The numbers are made up to show the shapes, not real prices.
